# Gridiron AI — session write-up (2026-10-02)

## The idea (his words)

"An AI that can analyze film for American football and break down:
tendencies offensive and defensive, key players, type of offense, type
of defense, plays to review, what could work against. Almost like how
Hudl does now but more advanced."

Full-fledged coach + analyst in one. It watches film and understands
what it's looking at.

## Audience ladder (his call)

- v1: for him (self-scout)
- v2: coaches
- v3: sell

## Architecture

- **Eyes:** vision model watches sampled frames per play — formation,
  motion, play type, result. No classic CV pipeline to start.
- **Brain:** formation/play knowledge base built from coaching resources
  online; retrieval matches what the eyes see to name formations,
  coverages, fronts.
- **Analyst:** tendencies, key players by impact, scheme ID,
  auto-clipped plays to review.
- **Coach:** counters ("what could work"), game-plan suggestions,
  plain-English Q&A over the film.

## His directives (the spec, in his words → implications)

1. **"Notice the backfield and the full play. Pre and post."** → Read
   all 11, pre-snap AND post-snap. Never a frozen frame of receivers.
2. **Personnel first:** count backs/receivers/TEs — 10/11/12/21/22.
   Personnel is the primary tendency key; everything hangs off it.
3. **Defense gets the same:** front ID (4-3, 3-4, nickel, dime) and the
   defensive answer to offensive personnel. The matchup is a tendency
   key.
4. **Track through motion:** pre-motion set + post-motion set (doubles →
   3x1 jet motion is itself a tell).
5. **Probabilistic coverage pre-snap**, confirmed post-snap via safety
   movement. Never lock a call on a frozen frame.

## Film tests

### Test #1 — Warner blind (test-01-warner-blind.md)

3 pre-snap All-22 frames, Kurt Warner QB Confidential. Warner never
named coverages (video was about throw difficulty) → labeled track
failed; blind reads only. His corrections:
- Play 1: machine said 3x1/Cover 2 → he said **2x2 doubles, man-free
  (Cover 1)**. Lesson: depth alone doesn't make a shell; count
  detached eligibles.
- Play 3: machine said 2x2/Cover 3 → he said **empty 3x2, maybe Cover 4
  depending on the safety**. Lesson: pre-snap shell ≠ coverage.

### Test #2 — QB School labeled (test-02-qbschool-labeled.md)

3 pre-snap All-22 frames, JT O'Sullivan "Rodgers vs Browns Week 4".
**Coverage: 3 for 3.**
- Play 1: O'Sullivan "man coverage" → machine Cover 1. Hit. (He added:
  starts doubles, motions to 3x1 — machine missed the pre-motion look.)
- Play 2: O'Sullivan named nothing (protection segment) → he confirmed
  machine's Cover 3. Hit. (He added: shotgun not under center, RB weak
  — machine never located the RB.)
- Play 3: O'Sullivan "middlefield closed, press man" → machine's lean
  Cover 1 (45%). Hit on the lean — validates the probabilistic format.

## Product gold (O'Sullivan, verbatim from transcript)

- "watch the motion from the back and watch the defense not move...
  that's **man indicator number one**" → encodable rule.
- "you really have to **see it post snap**... that's the modern game"
  → his pre/post directive, validated by an 11-year NFL QB.
- "it presents like middlefield closed man, but **it's really zero**"
  → track pre-snap presentation vs. post-snap reality separately
  (disguise detection).
- "the Browns this whole game were kind of giving **two middlefield
  closed looks**" → that one-line tendency sentence IS the product.

## Boundaries

- Hudl: no account sharing, no automation (their ToS bans both, plus
  using content to build competing/AI products). Path is he exports
  video himself, hands over the file.
- Free full-game All-22 doesn't exist (NFL+ paywall). YouTube
  breakdowns work for testing; his own film is the benchmark
  (ground truth: he knows what was called).
- Business side stays personal, same rule as Orion.
